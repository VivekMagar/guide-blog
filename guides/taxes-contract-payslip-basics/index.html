Taxes, Employment Contracts & Payslip Basics in Romania

Overview

In Romania, employment is governed by the Labor Code (Codul Muncii). Understanding the difference between your Gross Salary (Salariul Brut) and Net Salary (Salariul Net) is critical so you know exactly how much money should land in your bank account each month.

Gross vs. Net Salary: The Numbers

When recruiting agencies in Nepal promise salaries (e.g. "600 USD" or "3,000 RON"), always confirm whether that number is Gross or Net.

Simple Rule of Thumb: In Romania, an employee's net take-home salary is approximately 60% to 65% of the gross contract figure, unless working in tax-incentivized sectors like construction or agriculture ⚠️ [VERIFY: Check current status of tax exemptions in construction/agriculture under Fiscal Code amendments].

How to Read Your Payslip (Fluturaș de Salariu)

Your employer is legally obligated to give you a monthly payslip showing exact hours worked, overtime, and deductions.

Key Terms on Your Payslip

  1. Salariu de Bază / Brut Realizat: The base gross salary earned for the hours worked in the month.
  2. Ore Suplimentare: Overtime hours. Under Romanian law, overtime must be compensated either by paid time off or by an overtime wage bonus of at least ⚠️ [VERIFY: Minimum legal overtime percentage bonus under Codul Muncii art. 123, standard minimum 75% addition to base wage].
  3. Sporuri (Bonuses/Allowances): Night work allowance (at least 25% extra between 22:00 and 06:00), holiday work, or performance bonuses.
  4. Total Rețineri (Total Deductions): The sum of CAS, CASS, and Income Tax.
  5. Rest de Plată (Net Payout): The final cash amount that must be transferred to your bank IBAN.

Illegal Deductions & Scams to Watch Out For

Unlawful "Breakage" or "Penalty" Deductions

Employers cannot arbitrarily subtract money from your salary for broken dishes, slow production, or bad reviews. Under Article 169 of the Romanian Labor Code, salary deductions can only be made under strict conditions:

  • By court order, or
  • With your explicit written consent, or
  • When formal damages have been audited through statutory disciplinary procedures.

Working "Grey" Hours (Munca la Negru / Gri)

If an employer asks you to work 12 hours a day but only reports 8 hours on your contract, you are being deprived of overtime pay, retirement credits, and full health insurance coverage.

Filing a Complaint with Labor Authorities (ITM)

If your employer refuses to pay wages, confiscates documents, or forces illegal overtime:

  • You have the legal right to submit a complaint (Sesizare) to the Territorial Labor Inspectorate (ITM - Inspectoratul Teritorial de Muncă) in your county.
  • Filing an ITM complaint is free and protected by whistleblower protections under Romanian law.
