Overview

Arriving in Romania on a work visa (type D/AM) is the beginning of a major transition. The first 7 days set the foundation for your legal residency, safety, and financial wellbeing.

This checklist guides you through what you must do yourself, what your employer is legally obligated to provide, and how to protect yourself against document confiscation and unlawful deductions.

What to Prepare Before You Leave Bucharest Airport

Do not leave the airport arrivals terminal until you have verified your documents and secured basic communication.

CRITICAL LEGAL NOTICE: Your employer or agency representative has NO legal right to hold your physical passport. In Romania, retaining someone's identity documents against their will is a criminal offense under ⚠️ [VERIFY: Specific article of Romanian Penal Code regarding document retention / art. 282 or related]. You may give them a photocopy for human resources registration, but always keep your original passport with you.

Common Pitfalls & How to Avoid Them

1. Handing over your original passport

  • The Pitfall: Agents or employers ask to "keep your passport in the company safe for security."
  • How to Avoid: Provide a color photocopy or PDF. State politely: "I need my original passport for identity checks and personal banking."

2. Missing the residence permit application deadline

  • The Pitfall: The long-stay work visa (D/AM) is typically valid for ⚠️ [VERIFY: Exact validity period of Romanian D/AM work visa, usually 90 days from arrival]. If your residence permit (Permis de Ședere) application is not lodged with IGI at least ⚠️ [VERIFY: Statutory deadline before visa expiry to file IGI application, typically 30 days before visa expires], you may become unlawfully present.
  • How to Avoid: Ask your employer's HR within your first week: "When is our appointment scheduled with IGI?"

3. Paying unauthorized "accommodation fees" or salary deductions

  • The Pitfall: Employers deducting hundreds of Euros from your salary for housing or food without a signed annex.
  • How to Avoid: Any salary deduction in Romania requires explicit contractual basis or court order under the Romanian Labor Code (Codul Muncii art. 169). Review your payslip closely every month.
