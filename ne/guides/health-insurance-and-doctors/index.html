Health Insurance & Seeing a Doctor in Romania (CNAS & Healthcare)

📅 अन्तिम अपडेट: 2026-11-19 ⏱️ 5-7 min read

Overview

If you work legally in Romania on an individual employment contract (CIM), your employer deducts CASS (10%) from your gross pay every month. This mandatory contribution entitles you to public healthcare coverage through the National Health Insurance House (CNAS).

This guide explains how to access free or subsidized medical care, register with a family physician, and handle emergency hospital visits.

Emergency Medical Care: Free for Everyone (112 & UPU)

If you have a life-threatening illness, severe injury, high fever, or severe burn:

  1. Call 112: The unified emergency phone number in Romania is 112. It is free, works without SIM credit, and dispatchers speak Romanian and English.
  2. Go to Hospital Emergency Rooms (UPU / CPU): Emergency units (Unitatea de Primiri Urgențe) at public county hospitals must treat emergencies immediately without upfront payment, regardless of whether your insurance paperwork is complete or where you are from.
  3. What to Take: If possible, take your passport, residence permit or filing slip, and any medical records.

Routine & Non-Emergency Care: Step-by-Step

For non-emergency problems (back pain, dental pain, ongoing stomach issues, minor injuries), do not go to UPU. Follow this standard public healthcare process:

Step 1: Prove You Are Insured (Adeverință de Asigurat)

Ask your employer's HR or accounting department for an insurance certificate:

  • Document name: Adeverință de salariat pentru medic (Worker insurance certificate).
  • This confirms that your company has paid CASS contributions to the county health insurance house (CAS).

Step 2: Register with a Family Doctor (Medic de Familie)

The family doctor is your gatekeeper to public healthcare:

  • You may register with any family doctor in your neighborhood or city who has open patient slots.
  • The family doctor provides basic checkups and writes referral letters (Bilet de Trimitere) to hospital specialists.

Step 3: Specialist Consultations & Tests

  • With a referral letter (Bilet de Trimitere), visits to specialist clinics contracted with CNAS are free or covered under the state insurance quota.
  • Without a referral letter, private clinics charge commercial consultation fees (typically ⚠️ [VERIFY: Average private clinic general consultation price in RON, e.g. 150-300 RON]).

Medical Sick Leave (Concediu Medical)

If you are sick or injured and cannot work:

  1. Doctor's Medical Certificate: Only a licensed doctor (family doctor or hospital physician) can issue an official medical leave certificate (Certificat de Concediu Medical).
  2. Submit to Employer Within ⚠️ [VERIFY: Statutory deadline to submit medical leave certificate to employer, standard by the 5th of the following month]: Hand a copy to your employer's HR immediately so your absence is excused and paid.
  3. Sick Leave Pay: Sick leave is compensated at ⚠️ [VERIFY: Standard sick leave compensation percentage of base salary under OUG 158/2005, typically 75% for common illness, 100% for work accidents or emergency surgeries] of your average insured gross salary.
  4. Protection from Termination: While on documented medical leave, your employer cannot legally fire you under Article 60 of the Romanian Labor Code.

What to Prepare Before a Doctor Visit

Common Pitfalls & Mistakes

1. Waiting until an illness becomes severe

  • Many workers delay seeing a doctor because of language barriers or fear of employer anger. Early treatment prevents costly hospitalization.

2. Buying antibiotics over the counter without a prescription

  • In Romania, pharmacies (Farmacie) strictly cannot sell antibiotics without a doctor's prescription (Rețetă). Do not rely on unregulated private trade.

3. Missing workplace accident reporting

  • If you are injured on the job (construction, restaurant kitchen, factory), report it to your supervisor immediately. Workplace accidents (Accident de Muncă) have 100% state coverage and special protections, but only if officially registered in the safety log.
